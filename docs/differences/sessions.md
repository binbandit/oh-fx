# Saving, resuming, and naming sessions

- The interactive session saves its conversation to the session store as upstream does and remembers it for `-c` once its first turn is saved or, as upstream, once its first turn writes a recovery checkpoint, so a first turn paused with Esc, or one whose shell was killed while it waited to retry, is reopened by `-c`, and the checkpoint keeps the session when the shell exits. `-c` and `--continue` reopen the session remembered for the workspace; `resume`, `resume last`, `--resume`, `--resume last`, and `session resume` the newest session of the workspace; and `resume <id>`, `--resume <id>`, `--resume-<id>`, and `session resume <id>` that session, moving it to this workspace when it was saved in another, and remember it. A session that cannot be opened, or a session store that cannot be opened for any of these or for `-r`, ends the launch with upstream's message before the shell starts; a plain launch without a store runs without saving, as upstream does. A resumed session restores its model, reasoning effort, and Fast mode, except those its launch flags set, and shows its saved prompts, replies, answered questions, tool calls, cancellations, and failed turns in the scrollback under upstream's `session resumed` notice. Each tool call is a row, grouped as the live session groups them, with upstream's resume label: the completed or `Failed` action, the denial, `Not executed`, or project-instructions deferral its result records, or a subagent's outcome, still-running child, or delivered message. A row takes its label from the saved result's preview, and only a subagent's or a question's result, whose outcome is structured, is read whole from `tool-results`. As upstream, a failed row leaves out the detail the live row showed, which is not saved, a question tool call that was not answered shows as a row, and the call an interruption left running is left out. Command output and file diffs are not replayed under their rows yet: oh-fx's transcript shows neither under a tool row, and oh-fx saves no command replays or file presentations, though it keeps the command replays fx saved, as the session store differences record. A turn whose frame carries upstream's turn summary shows its duration and token row after it, as upstream's resume does, but oh-fx does not save turn summaries yet, so its own turns resume without that row. `--sessions-v2`, or `OH_FX_SESSIONS_V2` set to `1` or to `true` in any letter case, fails the interactive session as not available yet, as they do for `ask` without `--no-save`. The interactive session honors `--model`, `--effort`, `--fast`, and `--no-fast` given before any command, and validates and then ignores `--provider-order`, `--provider-strict`, and `--no-provider-strict`, as `ask` does.
- `/resume` and `-r` open upstream's session picker in the footer, in place of the status line: the sessions of the workspace, or of every workspace after Shift+Tab, newest first, each with its title, workspace, age, and turn count, filtered by the text typed in the composer, and loaded a page at a time, more as the selection reaches the end. Enter resumes the selected session without waiting for its lock, so a session open in another oh-fx shows as busy in the picker until it is closed there, and Escape closes the picker, starting a fresh session when `-r` opened it. Upstream's `This session is being updated` refusal, for a session whose storage is moving between upstream's session formats, is left out, since oh-fx keeps one session format. Super+R opens it over every workspace once the composer is empty, as upstream does. As upstream does, the listing runs in the background through the `.resume-catalog` index, the picker keeps it for five seconds so a reopen or a scope switch within that time reuses it, later pages always continue it, and the interactive session lists in the background at startup when the index exists. Upstream also paints a cold open from the saved index before that listing finishes; oh-fx waits for the listing instead, which takes about 9 ms for 2,000 sessions once the index exists (release build, macOS arm64, warm page cache), under one frame. A session resumed from the picker restores its model, reasoning effort, and Fast mode, except those its launch flags set, as upstream does. One saved with another provider is refused with a notice that names `oh-fx resume <id>` and stays in its workspace, because the shell connects to its provider once at launch; upstream switches the provider. `/resume` or Super+R during a turn or a manual compaction reports upstream's `resume is unavailable until the response finishes`; upstream refuses them only during a turn and opens the picker during a manual compaction. While the picker is open it owns the footer, as upstream's does: typed text, an `@` included, filters it, Enter, Tab, and the arrow and Ctrl+N keys go to it, Ctrl+P, the model menu shortcut, does nothing until it closes, and the `@` file picker stays closed until it closes.
- `oh-fx sessions` also lists the sessions upstream fx saved in `~/.fx/sessions`, so the history of someone moving from fx stays in view. They are listed beside oh-fx's own, newest first, within the same workspace and `--all` scopes and cursor pages, and each is marked `fx`: its text line ends with `| fx` and its JSON object carries `"source": "fx"`, which oh-fx's own sessions leave out. oh-fx only reads fx's folder: it writes, locks, and repairs nothing there, never saves fx's `.resume-catalog` index though it reuses the rows that still match, and lists nothing from it unless `~/.fx` and `~/.fx/sessions` are private (mode 0700) directories and not symlinks, as fx creates them. An fx session is not listed when oh-fx's store has a session folder of the same id, so a session saved in both lists only oh-fx's copy, which counts as unreadable when oh-fx cannot read it. An fx session oh-fx cannot read, such as one holding fields oh-fx does not accept yet, is left out unless a row of fx's index that still matches it lists it, and is never counted among the unreadable sessions, since `oh-fx doctor` checks only oh-fx's own store. Nothing else reads fx's folder: `ask`, `oh-fx doctor`, the session picker, `-c`, and `resume` neither list nor open fx sessions, so oh-fx cannot resume one yet. Upstream lists only its own sessions.
- `/clear`, `/new`, and `/reset` each start a fresh saved session, as upstream does, keeping the one they leave unless nothing, not even a `/model` or `/fast` choice or a title, was saved to it; leaving the shell keeps it the same way. They keep the AGENTS.md project instructions read when the interactive session started and forget the scoped rules added during the conversation, so instruction files edited since then are read again only by a new process. Upstream starts a fresh agent session for all three; at the synced commit `/reset` differs from `/clear` and `/new` only in a background-session policy that upstream discards, so oh-fx runs them alike.
- `/clear`, `/new`, and `/reset` empty the composer's kill ring and number the next pasted block from 1 again when the agent reports the conversation cleared, as upstream's session reset does. The agent clears the conversation on its own thread, so keys typed after any of them is submitted reach the composer before that report: a draft typed then stays, a kill made then is emptied with the ring, and a block pasted then keeps its number, so the next block is numbered after it. Upstream resets the session before it handles the next key, so there such a kill survives and such a block is numbered from 1.
- The interactive session and `ask` name a fresh saved session from its first prompt with the provider's title model, as upstream does: `gpt-5.6-luna` for Codex, and none for custom connections, so they are never named this way. The request runs beside the first turn, and the task that sent it installs the title as soon as it arrives, under the session's lock, unless the session it was sent for has closed, such as after `/clear`, or carries a title the user chose. Upstream hands the title to its UI loop, which drops it when the active session changed, and `ask` installs it after the run, so there a generated title can follow the first save instead of preceding it; both end with the generated title unless the user chose one. `/clear`, `/new`, and `/reset` stop a title request still running for the session they leave, so the fresh session's first prompt can send its own; upstream lets that request finish and drops its title, and until it finishes the fresh session's first prompt sends none, so that session is never named. `ask` cancels the request when a signal or a failed write cancels the run, where upstream waits for it, up to its 15-second deadline, before exiting. A failure leaves the session untitled silently, as upstream's does, but writes no trace line and keeps no last outcome, since oh-fx has no trace log or `/trace` yet. The reply is bounded by the transport's stream limits rather than upstream's 4 KiB content capture. `session_titles` is read from `settings.json` when a session starts, and the `/settings` menu's `Session titles` row switches it for the next title request and saves it, as upstream's does.
- A session's title shows in the `session resumed` notice, in the session picker, in `/rename`'s notice, and as the terminal's window title, which reads `oh-fx v<version> | <folder>` until the session has a title, as upstream's does: the saved or derived title of a resumed session, the first prompt's derived title once its turn is in the history, a generated title, or a `/rename` title, without control characters and cut to 128 bytes with `...`. Upstream also shows it in the status line's session segment and in `fx sessions`; those wait for the status line segment and the session commands. `/status` shows no title, as upstream's does not.
