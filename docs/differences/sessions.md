# Saving, resuming, and naming sessions

- The interactive session saves its conversation to the session store as upstream does and remembers it for `-c` once its first turn is saved or, as upstream, once its first turn writes a recovery checkpoint, so a first turn paused with Esc, or one whose shell was killed while it waited to retry, is reopened by `-c`, and the checkpoint keeps the session when the shell exits. `-c` and `--continue` reopen the session remembered for the workspace; `resume`, `resume last`, `--resume`, `--resume last`, and `session resume` the newest session of the workspace; and `resume <id>`, `--resume <id>`, `--resume-<id>`, and `session resume <id>` that session, moving it to this workspace when it was saved in another, and remember it. A session that cannot be opened, or a session store that cannot be opened for any of these or for `-r`, ends the launch with upstream's message before the shell starts; a plain launch without a store runs without saving, as upstream does. A resumed session restores its model, reasoning effort, and Fast mode, except those its launch flags set. One saved with a provider oh-fx cannot use, `gateway` (fx's default), `grok`, or a custom provider that oh-fx's settings do not define, continues with the provider and model oh-fx would start a new session with, says so in a notice that names the saved provider (`oh-fx ask:` on stderr for `ask`), and is saved with them, keeping its reasoning effort, Fast mode, and Ultra request. Upstream can use the gateway and Grok, and refuses a session whose custom provider its settings do not define with `UnknownConfiguredProvider`. A session whose custom provider is defined but has changed is refused with `ConfiguredProviderChanged`, as upstream does. A resumed session shows its saved prompts, replies, answered questions, tool calls, cancellations, and failed turns in the scrollback under upstream's `session resumed` notice. Each tool call is a row, grouped as the live session groups them, with upstream's resume label: the completed or `Failed` action, the denial, `Not executed`, or project-instructions deferral its result records, or a subagent's outcome, still-running child, or delivered message. A row takes its label from the saved result's preview, and only a subagent's or a question's result, whose outcome is structured, is read whole from `tool-results`. As upstream, a failed row leaves out the detail the live row showed, which is not saved, a question tool call that was not answered shows as a row, and the call an interruption left running is left out. Command output and file diffs are not replayed under their rows yet: oh-fx's transcript shows neither under a tool row, and oh-fx saves no command replays or file presentations, though it keeps the ones fx saved and a resumed edit row shows the path and line counts its presentation holds, as the session store differences record. A turn whose frame carries upstream's turn summary shows its duration and token row after it, as upstream's resume does, but oh-fx does not save turn summaries yet, so its own turns resume without that row. `--sessions-v2`, or `OH_FX_SESSIONS_V2` set to `1` or to `true` in any letter case, fails the interactive session as not available yet, as they do for `ask` without `--no-save`. The interactive session honors `--model`, `--effort`, `--fast`, and `--no-fast` given before any command, and validates and then ignores `--provider-order`, `--provider-strict`, and `--no-provider-strict`, as `ask` does.
- `/resume` and `-r` open upstream's session picker in the footer, in place of the status line: the sessions of the workspace, or of every workspace after Shift+Tab, newest first, each with its title, workspace, age, and turn count, filtered by the text typed in the composer, and loaded a page at a time, more as the selection reaches the end. Enter resumes the selected session without waiting for its lock, so a session open in another oh-fx shows as busy in the picker until it is closed there, and Escape closes the picker, starting a fresh session when `-r` opened it. Upstream's `This session is being updated` refusal, for a session whose storage is moving between upstream's session formats, is left out, since oh-fx keeps one session format. Super+R opens it over every workspace once the composer is empty, as upstream does. As upstream does, the listing runs in the background through the `.resume-catalog` index, the picker keeps it for five seconds so a reopen or a scope switch within that time reuses it, later pages always continue it, and the interactive session lists in the background at startup when the index exists. Upstream also paints a cold open from the saved index before that listing finishes; oh-fx waits for the listing instead, which takes about 9 ms for 2,000 sessions once the index exists (release build, macOS arm64, warm page cache), under one frame. A session resumed from the picker restores its model, reasoning effort, and Fast mode, except those its launch flags set, as upstream does. One saved with another provider is refused with a notice that names `oh-fx resume <id>` and stays in its workspace, because the shell connects to its provider once at launch; upstream switches the provider. One saved with a provider oh-fx cannot use continues on the shell's provider and model instead, with the notice `resume` shows. The picker also lists the fx sessions that `oh-fx sessions` lists, beside oh-fx's own and newest first, and marks each with `· fx` after its turn count, a column that appears only while an fx session is listed, so the rows keep upstream's layout otherwise; upstream's picker lists only its own sessions. The background listing, at startup included, reads them with oh-fx's own sessions, so the rows show once both are read, about 15 ms later for 200 fx sessions, as the fx listing timings below record. Enter on an fx row copies the session into oh-fx's store as `resume <id>` does and resumes the copy, or refuses with the import's message, such as when fx has the session open, and Enter on an imported copy refreshes it from fx as `resume <id>` does. `/resume` or Super+R during a turn or a manual compaction reports upstream's `resume is unavailable until the response finishes`; upstream refuses them only during a turn and opens the picker during a manual compaction. While the picker is open it owns the footer, as upstream's does: typed text, an `@` included, filters it, Enter, Tab, and the arrow and Ctrl+N keys go to it, Ctrl+P, the model menu shortcut, does nothing until it closes, and the `@` file picker stays closed until it closes.
- A resumed session restores the Ultra request its `session.json` saves (`ultrafast_mode`), whether `-c`, `resume`, or the picker opens it, unless the shell already holds an Ultra choice of its own, as upstream's process override does: one `--ultrafast`, `--no-ultrafast`, or `--fast` made at launch, or one made since by any `/ultrafast off`, even while no request was on, by turning Fast mode on, or by a model change that withdrew the request. A model, Fast mode, or `/ultrafast` change saves the request with the session's preferences, so `/ultrafast off` keeps it off on the next resume, and an effort change leaves the saved request as it was. oh-fx models have no Ultra mode, so the restored request only shows in `/ultrafast` and `/status` until the Vercel AI Gateway lands. Subagents start without it, where upstream's inherit it; that inheritance waits for the same transport, as [the parity record](../upstream-parity.md) notes for #1112.
- `oh-fx sessions` also lists the sessions upstream fx saved in `~/.fx/sessions`, so the history of someone moving from fx stays in view. They are listed beside oh-fx's own, newest first, within the same workspace and `--all` scopes and cursor pages, and each is marked `fx`: its text line ends with `| fx` and its JSON object carries `"source": "fx"`, which oh-fx's own sessions leave out. oh-fx only reads fx's folder: it writes, locks, and repairs nothing there, and lists nothing from it unless `~/.fx` and `~/.fx/sessions` are private (mode 0700) directories and not symlinks, as fx creates them. An fx session is not listed when oh-fx's store has a session folder of the same id, so a session saved in both lists only oh-fx's copy, which counts as unreadable when oh-fx cannot read it. An fx session is listed only when oh-fx could resume it: its `session.json` and every frame of its `events.jsonl` decode as oh-fx decodes them, the frames form a conversation oh-fx accepts, and a `recovery.json` saved for the event a resume would continue from decodes too. Others, such as sessions holding images, or a recovery checkpoint holding a file presentation, which oh-fx does not accept yet, are left out until oh-fx accepts them, and are never counted among the unreadable sessions, since `oh-fx doctor` checks only oh-fx's own store. Because fx's `.resume-catalog` index records what fx can read, oh-fx ignores it and reads every fx session on each listing, on four threads, keeping no index of its own: listing 200 fx sessions with 50 MB of logs takes about 15 ms, and 1,000 with 110 MB about 37 ms (release build, macOS arm64, warm page cache). `resume <id>`, `--resume <id>`, `--resume-<id>`, `session resume <id>`, and `ask --resume <id>` or `--resume-id <id>` resume an fx session that oh-fx's store does not hold by copying it into the store under the same id and resuming the copy, so it then lists as an oh-fx session. The copy takes `session.json`, `events.jsonl`, `tool-results`, `recovery.json`, `recovery.asked`, `permissions.json`, `usage-v2.json`, `images`, `logs`, and `artifacts`, and leaves out fx's `session.lock` and `owner.live`, its `history-cache.bin`, which the resume rebuilds, terminal state, ACP files, and subagent children, which stay with fx. While it copies, oh-fx holds a shared lock on fx's `session.lock`, opened without creating it, checks the session as the listing does, and builds the copy in a `creating+` folder that it syncs, locks as the session's writer, and renames into place without replacing anything, removing that folder if any step fails, as a failed session start does. It holds that lock until it has opened the copy and written the record below, so no other resume can save into the copy first, and a copy published without that record, which only an import that stopped in between leaves, is kept as oh-fx's own and never refreshed. It refuses, leaving nothing behind, while fx holds the session open, while fx has not finished rewriting the log (`events.jsonl.compact-tmp` or `events-compaction.pending` is present; the `events-compaction.marker` fx keeps afterwards is no obstacle), and when oh-fx could not resume the session. Once the copy exists, resuming the id opens it, except that it is replaced by a fresh copy when fx's `session.json` or `events.jsonl` changed since the import and the copy has no turns of its own. An `fx-import.json` file in the copy records fx's two files at the import (inode, size, and modification time) and, after the copy's first resume, which may close a turn fx left open, the length of the copy's log, its `recovery.json`, its title, and the provider, model, reasoning effort, Fast mode, and Ultra request in its `session.json`. A turn oh-fx saves, a paused turn, a rename, a subagent child, or a provider, model, reasoning effort, Fast mode, or Ultra choice saved in oh-fx therefore keeps the copy as it is, while a choice that leaves them as they were does not. Moving the copy to the current provider when fx saved it with one oh-fx cannot use updates the record too, unless the copy already holds work of its own, so a copy that was only opened keeps following fx. While fx holds the session open, a refresh is refused as an import is; when fx's newer session cannot be copied for another reason, such as data oh-fx cannot read yet, the copy is resumed as it is. fx's files are never changed. `-c` and `--continue` also read the session fx remembers for the workspace in `~/.fx/continue/<sha256 of the workspace root>`, which fx names and fills as oh-fx does its own. When oh-fx remembers no session for the workspace, they resume the one fx remembers, and when both remember one, the one used last, by the time the listing sorts by of the copy resume would open: oh-fx's copy of the session when there is one, and fx's original when there is none or the copy is an untouched import that fx has changed since, with a tie going to oh-fx's. fx's pointer counts only when `~/.fx` and `~/.fx/continue` are private directories and not symlinks, and the pointer is a private regular file with one link, at most 256 bytes, holding a session id and a newline, as oh-fx requires of its own; any other is ignored, never an error. When fx's pointer names a session oh-fx could not resume, including one whose copy in oh-fx is unreadable, the one oh-fx remembers is resumed instead, and when oh-fx remembers none, the launch fails with the reason, as `resume <id>` does; a session fx holds open is refused the same way when it is the one chosen. A session reached through fx's pointer is then remembered for the workspace in oh-fx's own pointer. `resume` and `resume last`, `--resume` and `--resume last`, `session resume`, and `ask --resume last` resume the newest session of the workspace in either store, counting the fx sessions the listing shows, and copy it in when it is fx's, so a newest fx session that fx holds open is refused as `resume <id>` refuses it, and one fx moves to another workspace before it is opened is skipped, as a moved oh-fx session is. The session picker reaches them too, as its bullet records, while `oh-fx doctor` does not reach fx sessions yet. Upstream reads only its own sessions.
- fx 0.0.1 to 0.0.7 saved sessions in an older schema-v3 layout: an `authority.json` marker, an `events.jsonl` of semantic events, and a `commit.<generation>.json` watermark naming the prefix of that log that was committed. fx 0.0.8 and later convert such a session to the conversation layout in place when they resume it, so sessions not opened since stay in the old layout. `oh-fx sessions`, the session picker, and every resume that reaches fx's current sessions reach them too. oh-fx reads the committed prefix as upstream's import does, ignoring later bytes and the `session.json` manifest and `checkpoint.json` projections beside the log. Resuming one converts it into the copy rather than copying fx's files: the copy's `events.jsonl` holds the conversation log upstream's import writes, with every tool result fx kept inline stored in `tool-results` and marked partial, file evidence without a path dropped, a tool call whose arguments were not valid JSON repaired into a failed result as upstream repairs it, and a background command turn kept as its historical record. Its `session.json` takes the saved provider, model, reasoning effort, Fast mode, Ultra request, workspaces, and times, and the title of the first prompt. `tool-results`, `images`, `logs`, and `artifacts` are copied as for a current session. The listing takes its title from fx's `display.json`, as upstream's listing does, and the import record keeps no `session.json` stamp when fx saved no manifest. Upstream's import also writes `permissions.json` and the usage the log records to `usage-v2.json`; oh-fx writes neither, as it writes neither for its own sessions yet, and checks only that each usage snapshot in the log is a JSON object. Such a session is listed and imported only when its log holds no recovery checkpoint or state replacement, which those releases wrote for paused turns, compactions, and log rewrites, and its turns hold no images, file presentations, command replays, provider replays, review feedback, terminal actions, or compaction cancellations. A session behind an unfinished upgrade (`authority.pending.json`) is hidden too. Each stays hidden until oh-fx converts it. fx's files are never changed, and nothing converts in place.
- `/clear`, `/new`, and `/reset` each start a fresh saved session, as upstream does, keeping the one they leave unless nothing, not even a `/model` or `/fast` choice or a title, was saved to it; leaving the shell keeps it the same way. They keep the AGENTS.md project instructions read when the interactive session started and forget the scoped rules added during the conversation, so instruction files edited since then are read again only by a new process. Upstream starts a fresh agent session for all three; at the synced commit `/reset` differs from `/clear` and `/new` only in a background-session policy that upstream discards, so oh-fx runs them alike.
- `/clear`, `/new`, and `/reset` empty the composer's kill ring and number the next pasted block from 1 again when the agent reports the conversation cleared, as upstream's session reset does. The agent clears the conversation on its own thread, so keys typed after any of them is submitted reach the composer before that report: a draft typed then stays, a kill made then is emptied with the ring, and a block pasted then keeps its number, so the next block is numbered after it. Upstream resets the session before it handles the next key, so there such a kill survives and such a block is numbered from 1.
- The interactive session and `ask` name a fresh saved session from its first prompt with the provider's title model, as upstream does: `gpt-5.6-luna` for Codex, and none for custom connections, so they are never named this way. The request runs beside the first turn, and the task that sent it installs the title as soon as it arrives, under the session's lock, unless the session it was sent for has closed, such as after `/clear`, or carries a title the user chose. Upstream hands the title to its UI loop, which drops it when the active session changed, and `ask` installs it after the run, so there a generated title can follow the first save instead of preceding it; both end with the generated title unless the user chose one. `/clear`, `/new`, and `/reset` stop a title request still running for the session they leave, so the fresh session's first prompt can send its own; upstream lets that request finish and drops its title, and until it finishes the fresh session's first prompt sends none, so that session is never named. `ask` cancels the request when a signal or a failed write cancels the run, where upstream waits for it, up to its 15-second deadline, before exiting. A failure leaves the session untitled silently, as upstream's does, but writes no trace line and keeps no last outcome, since oh-fx has no trace log or `/trace` yet. The reply is bounded by the transport's stream limits rather than upstream's 4 KiB content capture. `session_titles` is read from `settings.json` when a session starts, and the `/settings` menu's `Session titles` row switches it for the next title request and saves it, as upstream's does.
- A session's title shows in the `session resumed` notice, in the session picker, in `/rename`'s notice, and as the terminal's window title, which reads `oh-fx v<version> | <folder>` until the session has a title, as upstream's does: the saved or derived title of a resumed session, the first prompt's derived title once its turn is in the history, a generated title, or a `/rename` title, without control characters and cut to 128 bytes with `...`. Upstream also shows it in the status line's session segment and in `fx sessions`; those wait for the status line segment and the session commands. `/status` shows no title, as upstream's does not.
